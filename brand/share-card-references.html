calibrEAT — share-card references

Six share-image systems worth stealing from, researched from design-team write-ups and design press. The little visual under each name is a hand-drawn hint at its style, not a mockup. For whoever picks up the redesign.

THE FIVE LESSONS THAT KEEP REPEATING

  1. One iconic object, not a screenshot. Apple's rings, Strava's route line — the image is a logo of the behavior, consistent colors every single time.
  2. Data is the hero; decoration is primitive. Strava's YIS system is built from "lines, dots, colour strips, circles, and typography" — nothing else.
  3. Story-format native, one stat per beat. Wrapped wins because it looks like a story, not a spreadsheet — superlatives ("top 0.5%") create status.
  4. Milestones are share moments. Duolingo fires the share card at 7 / 30 / 100 / 365 with milestone art — a reason to share that isn't "look at my dashboard".
  5. Pick a tone lane. Whoop is clinical black-data; Oura and Gentler Streak are warm and kind. Both win; the middle loses.

Strava — activity share card

CATEGORY BENCHMARK

Post-workout image: the route map is the hero, three stats underneath, brand orange on dark. People redesign it voluntarily because the original "looks like a screenshot".

  • The hero graphic is the app's fingerprint — our equivalent is the dial or the True Burn sparkline vs. formula line.
  • Share lives one tap from the moment (inside the activity), not buried in settings.
  • Third parties flourish making it "clean and minimalist" — that's the polish bar users already expect.

Strava — Year in Sport

DESIGN-SYSTEM GOLD

The annual recap, designed by consultancy Manual: a chaptered narrative (big picture → hard-won middle → triumph), data elevated as hero, palette lifted from race bibs and apparel then "toned down with navy and olive".

  • "Lines, dots, colour strips, circles, and typography" — the whole system is primitives, which is why it animates and scales.
  • Works for both extremes: celebratory for the 366-activity year and dignified for the 3-activity year. Our card must not humiliate a 2-day streak.
  • A campaign tagline gives shares a voice: "Athletes don't quit" → ours could be "measured, not guessed".

Spotify Wrapped

THE VIRAL MECHANIC

"Looks like an Instagram story, not a spreadsheet": bold color blocks, one personal stat per slide, motion, and superlative framing that turns data into identity.

  • Superlatives create status — "6/7 weeks at 90%+ logging" beats "6/7 days logged".
  • Sequence, not single frame: a 3-slide story (streak → burn drift → the equation) shares better than one dense card.
  • Natively composed for 9:16 — never a cropped dashboard.

Apple Fitness — Activity rings

MOST RECOGNIZABLE OBJECT

The rings are so consistent that the shape alone signals Apple Fitness — people recognize the share image from across a room, and some tattoo it.

  • Fixed colors + fixed geometry = brand memory. Our dial should never change palette between templates.
  • The object can carry a card alone — rings huge, text minimal. A dial-only "today" card is one build away.
  • Awards and limited-edition badges give the object narrative without comparing users.

Gentler Streak

THE KIND ALTERNATIVE

Apple Design Award winner built on the opposite of push: painterly illustration, a mascot ("your heart would be telling you"), and stats reframed as feelings — "needs met perfectly".

  • Interpretation over numbers: "Statistics are just numbers. Without knowing how to interpret them, they are meaningless."
  • Progress vs. your own history, never vs. other people — matches our no-leaderboard stance exactly.
  • Their share sheet offers light / dark / transparent backgrounds — user choice, same object. Validates a small template set.
Apple Behind the Design Sketch interview

Whoop / Oura — the two dark lanes

TONE CALIBRATION

The two poles of dark-UI health sharing: Whoop is intense, monospace, clinical; Oura is warm gradients, circles, encouraging words. A widely-cited comparison puts it simply: "Whoop is intense… Oura is gentle."

  • Both work; the middle is mush. Our Instrument/Terminal templates lean Whoop; nothing yet leans Oura.
  • Whoop shows one number + one meter — maximum confidence through restraint.
  • Oura frames the week as a "health story" — narrative titles ("A steadier week") over raw stats.

Duolingo — streak milestones

SHARE = RITUAL

The design team's own write-up: milestone art (the phoenix "Fire Duo") exists to make milestones celebratory and — explicitly — shareable, with a card "that allows you to easily save, post, or #humblebrag". They even redesigned the flame after finding it wasn't universally understood.

  • Fire the share card at milestones — 7 / 30 / 100 days is the moment people want to brag, and it's free motivation.
  • Test the metaphor: "on fire" isn't universal. Language on our cards should be plain ("12-day logging streak", not jargon).
  • The card is one piece of a system (animation, widget, reminder) — a share asset that only exists in settings gets shared never.
Duolingo design post Habit research
How to read this against our current four templates: Instrument and Terminal already sit in the Strava/Whoop lane (dark, data-hero) and follow lesson 2 well. The gaps the research points at are lesson 1 (no single iconic object yet — the dial could be it), lesson 3 (no story-format sequence or superlative framing), lesson 4 (milestone variants), and a warm lane option (Gentler/Oura) that would widen the audience beyond data people. Copy rules stay fixed either way: behavior, never bodies.
